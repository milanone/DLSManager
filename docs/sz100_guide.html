Horiba SZ-100: size measurements

Measurement settings, meaning of the reported quantities, forms of distribution, recalculation, and the corresponding functions of DLSManager. The complete operating procedure, with screenshots, is given in HORIBA's Quick Start: Making a Size Measurement (December 2011).

1. Latex check standard

The instrument is usually checked with a nominal 100 nm polystyrene latex.

  • Dispersion medium. 10 mM NaCl (or another 1:1 electrolyte) in deionised water, filtered through a 0.1 or 0.2 µm membrane to remove dust.
  • Dilution. One drop (about 30 µL) of the 1% v/v stock suspension in 10 mL of filtered medium. The stock bottle is mixed beforehand, its tip checked and the first drop discarded. The dilution is capped and swirled gently.
  • Inspection. A suspension with visible particles is discarded.
  • Cell. 1 to 2 mL in a clean square cell (minimum volume 1 mL).

2. Measurement settings

  • Temperature. Temperature control on, set to the measurement temperature (usually 25 °C). The sample is allowed a few minutes to equilibrate.
  • Sample. Particle material (mono-polystyrene, the default, for a latex) and dispersion medium. The viscosity and refractive index of the medium enter the calculated size and must be correct.
  • Measurement. General mode, manual scan of 120 s, 3 consecutive measurements without delay. Detector position 90° or 173° (backscattering); automatic ND filter.
  • Cell. The cuvette type is recorded with the measurement and does not enter the analysis.
  • Calculation. Monodisperse and Standard are recommended for a latex (section 4); the result is calculated on the scattered light intensity (section 5).
  • Saving. Automatic saving after each measurement, with sample name and sequential number. A sample name containing a character not allowed in Windows file names (\ / : * ? " < > |) is not saved.

Cumulants

The decay rate Γ of the correlogram gives the diffusion coefficient D = Γ/q², where q is the scattering vector; the size follows from the Stokes–Einstein relation d = kT / (3πηD).

  • Z-average: intensity-weighted mean size.
  • PI (polydispersity index): relative width of the distribution of decay rates.
  • Cumulants S.D.: Z-average × √PI. It is not an independent result.

Histogram

Mean, S.D., Mode and percentiles are the statistics of the size table. The width of the histogram is set by the form of distribution (section 4) and not by the PI: in the Monodisperse Standard form the S.D. is about one quarter of the mean for any sample. The peak lies close to the Z-average, generally slightly above it, and further above it for broad samples.

Two standard deviations are therefore reported. The Cumulants S.D. derives from the PI and reflects the measured decay; the histogram S.D. derives from the form of distribution and is an assumption. Polydispersity is to be judged from the PI and the Cumulants S.D.

The recomputed distribution and its S.D.

The Recomputed source of DLSManager inverts the stored correlogram without imposing a form of distribution. The weights p are obtained on 100 diameters d, equally spaced in ln d from 10 to 3000 nm:

  • mean = Σ(d·p) / Σp;
  • S.D. = √[ Σ p·(d − mean)² / Σp ].

Both refer to the selected weighting (intensity, volume or number), after the selected Width and tail cut. For the models with one or two peaks they refer to the sum of the peaks.

The width of the recomputed distribution follows the PI closely, although no formula links the two. The regularised inversion tends to broaden narrow samples, so that its S.D. is usually slightly larger than √PI × mean. It can differ considerably from the S.D. of the instrument's histogram, whose width is fixed; Width = Standard sets the recomputed distribution to the instrument's width. The Cumulants S.D. is reported for the instrument's results only (Parameters tab).

4. Forms of distribution

The instrument software calculates the size distribution under a form of distribution chosen by the operator, which fixes the width of the reported peaks. A stored measurement can be recalculated with another form (section 6). The form has two parts:

  • Dispersity: Monodisperse (one peak) or Polydisperse (one or more peaks).
  • Width: Broad (wide distributions), Standard (general use), Narrow (very narrow samples) or Manual (a resolution from −1 to 1).

The correlogram is fitted about equally well with each of these widths: the data do not determine which one applies. A broad or bimodal sample (high PI) can give two peaks in the Polydisperse form, whereas the Monodisperse form always gives one.

The .nsz file stores the resolution of the measurement as Dist_Resolution (0.3: Monodisperse Standard).

5. Advanced calculation options

  • Representation of the result: scattered light intensity (primary result), volume or number. Volume and number are converted with the Rayleigh approximation or with Mie theory (Mie or Averaged Mie). The Rayleigh approximation is suited to particles up to about 100 nm.
  • Threshold q: 5% by default.
  • Calculation range: 0.15 to 4000 nm (radius).
  • Correlation channels: automatic or user-defined ranges for the histogram and for the cumulants. The cumulant range is reported in the exports.
  • Particles outside the measurement range: their contribution is removed by default.

6. Recalculation and CSV export

A stored measurement is recalculated from Edit → Recalculate with another form or representation. The correlogram, Z-average, PI, Cumulants S.D. and count rate are unchanged. The recalculation produces a CSV file only; no new .nsz file is written.

Contents of the CSV file:

  • settings: angle, medium, refractive indices, form of distribution and dispersity, representation, count rate;
  • results: area, mean, S.D. and mode of each peak, Z-average, PI, cumulant range;
  • size table: 84 bins with frequency and undersize;
  • fit residuals, correlation function and fit.

The form used for a recalculation is recorded only in the CSV header (Distribution Form and Distribution Form(Dispersity)). DLSManager opens these files like .nsz files and shows correlation, fit, size table, results and form. The Recomputed source is not available for them, because the CSV does not contain the raw correlogram.

The diameters of the size table are the upper edges of the bins (bin ratio 1.1298). The mean and mode stated by the software refer to the bin centres and are about 6% lower than the values read from the plotted table.

8. Interpretation of results

  • Z-average and PI are the quantities that depend least on assumptions about the sample.
  • The width of the instrument's histogram is a property of the selected form, not a measurement of the sample.
  • Volume and number distributions depend strongly on the width of the intensity distribution and on the refractive index of the particle; they are to be regarded as indicative.
  • Values obtained with different forms of distribution are not directly comparable: the form is to be stated with the results.
