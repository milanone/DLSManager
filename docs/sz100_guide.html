Horiba SZ-100: settings and recalculation

A short guide written for DLSManager. It summarises, in its own words, the parts of the instrument's Quick Start (December 2011) that decide how a .nsz file or a CSV export should be read, and adds what was measured on real files. For the procedure in full, with the screens, the original manual is the reference.

1. A latex check standard

A nominal 100 nm polystyrene latex is the usual check of the instrument. The salt solution (10 mM NaCl, or another 1:1 electrolyte, in deionised water) is passed through a 0.1 or 0.2 µm filter first, to take out dust. The stock suspension is about 1% v/v: the bottle is mixed, its tip is checked, one drop is discarded, and one drop (about 30 µL) goes into 10 mL of the filtered salt solution, which is capped and swirled gently. A suspension in which particles are visible is discarded. One to two millilitres go into a clean square cell (1 mL is the minimum).

2. Settings of a size measurement

  • Temperature. The temperature control is switched on and set to the measurement temperature (25 °C in the files seen here); the sample then needs a few minutes to equilibrate, which is the time to set up the measurement.
  • Sample. Particle: mono-polystyrene (the factory default) for a latex; dispersion medium: water, or the right medium from the list. The viscosity and the refractive index of the medium must be correct: they enter the size.
  • Measurement. General mode; manual scan of 120 s; 3 consecutive measurements with no delay. The detector position is set to manual at 90° (the other choice is 173°, backscattering); the ND filter is automatic.
  • Cell. The cuvette type is recorded with the measurement and is not used in the analysis.
  • Calculation. Monodisperse and Standard are the recommended choices for a latex (section 4); the result is calculated on the scattered light intensity (section 5).
  • Saving. Automatic saving after each measurement, with the sample name and sequential numbers. A sample name with a character that Windows does not allow in a file name (\ / : * ? " < > |) is not saved.

A measurement yields one measured curve, the correlogram. Every other number is calculated from it, from conditions that are typed in, or from a sensor. The numbers that look alike come from different calculations, and it matters which.

The cumulants

The decay rate of the correlogram gives the diffusion coefficient D = Γ/q², with q the scattering vector, and the size follows from the Stokes–Einstein relation d = kT / (3πηD). The Z-average is the mean size obtained this way, weighted by the scattered intensity; the PI is the relative width of the decay rates. The Cumulants S.D. is not a separate result: it equals Z-average × √PI, exactly, in all 28 files that carry the cumulants and in the CSV exports checked (the 29th file is a blank with no sample).

The histogram

Mean, S.D., Mode and the percentiles are the statistics of the plotted table, calculated like those of any table. Its width is set by the form (section 4), not by the PI: the S.D. of the histogram is about a quarter of its mean whatever the sample, while the Cumulants S.D. follows the PI. Its position stays near the Z-average (ratio 1.02–1.09 in the files seen, 1.38 for the broad 450 nm liposomes); the rule that places it was not reconstructed.

Two standard deviations are therefore printed: the one of the cumulants (from the PI, a measurement of the width of the decay) and the one of the histogram (from the form, an assumption). For polydispersity the PI and the Cumulants S.D. are the quantities to read. A width taken from the PI instead of the form (σ = √PI) was tried on the 29 files: it does not approach the instrument's volume and number, nor the 130 nm latex, better than the Standard form.

The recomputed distribution and its S.D.

The source Recomputed of DLSManager inverts the stored correlogram itself, without the instrument's forms. Its mean and S.D. are those of the table of the distribution: with weights p on a grid of 100 diameters d equally spaced in ln d from 10 to 3000 nm, the mean is Σ(d·p) / Σp and the S.D. is √[ Σ p·(d − mean)² / Σp ], in nm. They are given in the weighting chosen (intensity, volume or number), after the width chosen in Width and the tail cut; for the models of one or two peaks they are those of the sum of the peaks on the grid.

The width of this distribution is the one the data allow: it follows the PI (over 28 files the relative S.D., S.D./mean, correlates 0.96 with √PI of the program's own cumulants, and 0.71 with the instrument's), but it is not tied to it by a formula. The regularised inversion tends to widen a narrow sample, so its S.D. is usually a little above √PI × mean (median relative S.D. 0.39 against √PI 0.34). The instrument's histogram in the Monodisperse Standard form has instead a relative S.D. of about 0.23–0.27 whatever the PI, so the two S.D. printed under a plot can differ by a factor of 1.5 (for instance 62 against 38 nm): choosing Width = Standard sets the recomputed one to the instrument's width. The program does not recompute the cumulants' S.D.; it is only shown for the instrument (Parameters tab).

4. Forms of distribution

The size distribution is not a free inversion of the correlogram: the software computes it under a form chosen by the user, which fixes the width of what it shows. The form has two parts, and the result can be recalculated later with another one (section 6).

  • Dispersity: Monodisperse (one peak) or Polydisperse (the software may show several peaks).
  • Width: Broad (for wide distributions), Standard (general use), Narrow (narrow samples, such as a polystyrene latex standard) or Manual, a resolution from −1 to 1 typed by the user.

The fit of the correlogram is equally good in all of them (residual about 10⁻³), so the data do not choose the width: a narrow or a broad form is an assumption about the sample, and the width shown says nothing about its polydispersity. In the Polydisperse form the 450 nm extruded liposomes (PI 0.38) give two peaks, 157 nm (39% of the intensity) and 611 nm (61%), where the Monodisperse form gives one peak at about 410 nm.

The .nsz files store the resolution used as Dist_Resolution: 0.3 in every file seen, i.e. Monodisperse Standard.

5. Advanced calculation options

  • Representation of the result: scattered light intensity (the one a measurement is made in), volume or number. Volume and number can be converted with the Rayleigh approximation or with Mie theory (the plain or an averaged Mie); the Rayleigh conversion is meant for particles of about 100 nm or less.
  • Threshold q: 5% by default.
  • Calculation range: 0.15 to 4000 nm (radius).
  • Channels of the correlation function: automatic for the histogram and for the cumulants, or a chosen range (1–41 here). The cumulant range is printed in the exports.
  • Particles outside the measurement range: their influence is eliminated by default.

6. Recalculation and the CSV export

The software recalculates a stored measurement from Edit → Recalculate, with another form or another basis. The correlogram does not change: a recalculation produces only a CSV with the new distribution and its fit, not a new .nsz. Z-average, PI, the cumulants' S.D. and the count rate are the same as in the original measurement.

The CSV holds, in order: the settings (angle, medium, refractive indices, form of distribution and dispersity, representation of the result, count rate); the results (area, mean, S.D. and mode of each peak, Z-average, PI, cumulant range); the size table (84 bins with frequency and undersize); the residual of the fit; and the correlation function with the fit.

The diameters of the size table are the upper edges of the bins (the bin ratio is 1.1298): the mean stated by the software equals the mean computed from the table divided by √1.1298 in every file checked, so its stated mean and mode sit about 6% below the peak drawn from the table.

8. What is verified

  • The widths of the forms, the presets of the manual and the two-peak result come from seven recalculated size tables (latex, 100 nm and 450 nm liposomes) and the instrument's dialogs; the map from resolution to width (the Manual choice) is an interpolation through four points.
  • Not known: the algorithm behind the forms, and which fields of the .nsz a recalculation would change (it does not write one).
  • The widths are properties of the instrument's presets, not measurements of the sample: for polydispersity, the PI and the correlogram itself are the quantities to look at.
